The Advance Booking Countdown

The single most trip-ruining failure in Alaska isn't weather or cost — it's arriving having booked nothing, and finding the Denali seats, the ferry deck, and the good campsites already sold to people who understood the calendar. Work down this page from the top. It's built to print (Ctrl/Cmd + P).

🖨 Print this calendar
Dates and windows move. The windows below were current when the book was written, and every one of them can shift year to year. Treat this page as the order of operations, and confirm each live before you count on it — the Official Links page goes straight to the source, and the Updates page tracks changes since printing.

The countdown, furthest out first

1–2 years outthe truly scarce

The most sought-after wilderness lodges

A small number of experiences run on very long horizons: the most in-demand lodges deep in Denali and a handful of remote backcountry operations can book a year or more ahead for peak week. Some in-park lodging across Alaska's parks opens its window nearly two years out. If your heart is set on one specific remote lodge in the last week of July, you are planning a year out, not months.

9–12 months outcruises & cruisetours

Book the sailing and the cabin category you actually want

Cruise lines announce and open their Alaska seasons far in advance. Booking this early gets you the itinerary, the cabin category, and the sailing date you want, plus the lower shoulder-season fares before they climb as the ship fills. One-way sailings (Vancouver–Whittier or Vancouver–Seward), the kind that hand you straight into a land trip, are fewer than round trips and go first. Cruisetours (the packaged cruise-plus-land extension) open on the same horizon, and the popular Denali-area lodge dates go first — you don't have to buy the packaged version, but if you want theirs, book it with the cruise.

~6 months outmid-February · the ferry & Denali

The ferry and Denali's own reservation system both open in February

The Alaska Marine Highway summer schedule has opened for booking in mid-February in recent years, covering May–September sailings. Vehicle space on the popular Inside Passage runs sells out, so any ferry leg — especially with a vehicle — is a February booking, not a spring one.

Denali's own reservation system opens for the season in the same mid-February window: bus seats (transit and narrated tour buses) and the park's reservable campgrounds, through the park concessioner. Only a majority of shuttle seats sell in advance, with the rest held back and released two days before each departure — don't gamble your one Denali day on that pool in peak season. With the full Park Road reopening, treat the popular full-length tour dates as first-to-sell.

~6 months out, rollingfederal campgrounds

Book the exact date, six months ahead, at 10:00am Eastern

Most federal campgrounds (national park, national forest, BLM) book through Recreation.gov on a rolling six-month window, with new dates releasing daily at 10:00am Eastern (6:00am Alaska time). To camp on a given July date, be online exactly six months before it — popular sites can go within minutes. Private RV parks near the marquee stops (Denali, Seward, the Kenai towns) want 3–6 months of lead time for summer; the RV doesn't free you from reservations, it changes what you're reserving.

3–6 months outflightseeing & marquee tours

Book the aircraft-based experiences earliest

Flightseeing, glacier landings, helicopter tours, and guided day tours book up months out for peak dates. In the cruise ports the pattern holds: remote, aircraft-based experiences (helicopter glacier landings, floatplane trips) want the longest lead — commonly 6–8 weeks, more in peak July — while in-town, water-based, and cultural tours can often be secured a few weeks out. If a helicopter glacier landing or a fly-in bear-viewing day is the one you'd be heartbroken to miss, book it months ahead, not as a port-day whim.

One worked timeline

A May 2027 Inside Passage cruise, one-way to Whittier, then land days through Anchorage and Denali on the newly whole road:

WhenWhat you do
As soon as dates firm (2026)Book the cruise, to lock the one-way sailing and the shoulder fare.
Mid-February 2027, week 1Book any ferry leg the moment the summer schedule opens; reserve Denali bus seats and campground as the park's season opens; grab Anchorage and Denali-area lodging.
February–March 2027Book federal campgrounds as each date rolls into its six-month window.
3–6 months outLock the one or two flightseeing or glacier tours you refuse to miss.
Everything elseMuseums, town walks, casual meals — left for the trip. None of it sells out.

Do it in that order and the trip assembles cleanly. Do it the month before, and the good pieces are gone.

The mistake first-timers make here

Treating "book by" as one date instead of a staggered series of windows. The things that sell out do not sell out on the same schedule — missing any one window can quietly remove the best part of your trip.

Your personal checklist

Check these off as you book. This section is designed to print cleanly on its own.

  • Cruise / cruisetour booked (9–12 months out)
  • Ferry leg booked, mid-February (if any leg of the trip uses the Alaska Marine Highway)
  • Denali bus seats + campground reserved, mid-February
  • Federal campgrounds booked, exactly 6 months ahead at 10:00am ET / 6:00am AK
  • Private RV park(s) booked, 3–6 months out
  • Flightseeing / glacier / helicopter tour booked, 6–8 weeks out (earlier for peak July)
  • Remote wilderness lodge booked, if applicable (a year or more out)
  • Rental car / RV reserved
  • America the Beautiful pass purchased or confirmed (see Official Links)
